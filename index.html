PhD abroad · A field guide

PhD Abroad: What I’ve Learned.

Thinking about a PhD abroad? You’re welcome here. These practical notes share what I’m learning along the way, for anyone exploring doctoral research internationally—whatever your college, degree, discipline or starting point.

01 / Finding opportunities⌕

Explore funded PhD positions

You can start by exploring research-group pages, doctoral-school vacancies, funded project listings and researcher networks. As you find interesting opportunities, compare the project, funding, eligibility and deadlines to see what feels right for you.

Project fit · Funding · Eligibility
02 / Contacting supervisors✉

Introduce yourself to a supervisor

A first email doesn’t need to be perfect. Share a little about your interests, mention what draws you to their work, and explain how your experience might connect. A focused CV and a short, personal message are a great place to begin.

Personal · Clear · Research-led
03 / Application materials▤

Tell your research story in your CV

Your CV is a chance to show how you’ve grown as a researcher. Include your education, thesis, projects, methods, field or laboratory skills, presentations and publications where relevant. It’s absolutely fine to be honest about what you know and what you’re excited to learn.

CV · Motivation letter · References
04 / Moving abroad↗

Get ready for your new chapter

Moving countries can feel like a lot to figure out, so take it one step at a time. Learn about visa or residence procedures, housing, insurance, salary or stipend, living costs and university onboarding. The details vary by country and institution, and it’s okay to ask questions along the way.

Relocation · Budget · Research culture
A note on this guide
There is no single route into a PhD.

Wherever you’re starting from, your path is worth exploring. Your university is one part of your story, alongside your curiosity, skills, experiences and the projects that excite you. I’m sharing what I learn as I go, including the questions I’ve had myself. I hope these notes make the process feel a little more approachable; requirements and experiences can differ between countries and institutions.
